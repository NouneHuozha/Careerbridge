# Fields Catalogue Revised Draft

## Review status

This revision keeps the existing 13 canonical field IDs and six-section structure. It is draft material only: proposed metadata remains `publication_status: draft`, `data_classification: real`, and `verification_status: pending_review`. No section below authorises a database update or publication.

All six sections in every field are **CareerBridge-authored educational guidance** unless a sentence is explicitly marked **Source-backed context**. Course families are examples to explore, not evidence of availability, eligibility, approval, recognition, fees, admission, employment or earnings.

**Sources checked:** 2026-11-20 UTC. Exact claim-to-source scope and evidence limits are recorded in [fields-source-remediation-matrix.md](./fields-source-remediation-matrix.md).

## Revised fields

### technology

- **Introduction:** Technology is about understanding, building, using and improving digital tools and systems.
- **What it broadly involves:** Software, devices, networks, data and the ways people use digital services.
- **Activities and areas of work:** Designing an app, testing a website, supporting a network, organising data and checking how a digital service works.
- **Learning and subject connections:** Mathematics, logical problem-solving, computing where available, communication and careful testing can be useful foundations.
- **Course families to explore:** Computer applications, computer science, information technology, electronics, digital skills and technical diplomas.
- **Further exploration:** Compare the actual subjects, practical work, delivery mode and entry requirements for a named programme. **Source-backed context:** AICTE publishes approval processes for applicable technical institutions and programmes; check the current provider and programme rather than assuming one rule applies everywhere.

### engineering

- **Introduction:** Engineering uses science, mathematics and design to work on practical structures, machines, products and systems.
- **What it broadly involves:** Asking how something works, defining a problem, testing ideas and improving a solution.
- **Activities and areas of work:** Designing a component, checking a structure, planning a system, using technical drawings, building prototypes and improving a process.
- **Learning and subject connections:** Mathematics, science, design, drawing, practical problem-solving and teamwork are useful areas to explore.
- **Course families to explore:** Engineering degrees, polytechnic or engineering diplomas, technical vocational programmes and specialised branches.
- **Further exploration:** Look at a named branch and institution before drawing conclusions about laboratories, admission tests or eligibility. **Source-backed context:** AICTE approval requirements apply to applicable technical institutions/programmes, not automatically to every course with an engineering-related name.

### healthcare

- **Introduction:** Healthcare includes many ways of supporting health, care, prevention, treatment and health services.
- **What it broadly involves:** Learning about people, health, science, care practices and the systems that help communities access services.
- **Activities and areas of work:** Health education, laboratory work, clinical support, rehabilitation, public health, records, technology and service coordination.
- **Learning and subject connections:** Biology and other sciences can be relevant for some routes; communication, observation, responsibility and teamwork matter across many roles.
- **Course families to explore:** Medical, nursing, pharmacy, allied-health, laboratory, public-health and health-support programmes, depending on the exact role.
- **Further exploration:** Regulated routes have different current requirements. **Source-backed context:** NMC covers medical undergraduate education; INC publishes nursing programme guidance; PCI regulates pharmacy education. Check the exact course, institution and applicable regulator before relying on an eligibility or registration claim.

### business

- **Introduction:** Business and management explores how organisations use people, information, money and time to provide products or services.
- **What it broadly involves:** Planning work, understanding customers, organising resources, communicating clearly and making informed decisions.
- **Activities and areas of work:** Accounting, operations, entrepreneurship, marketing, sales, finance, logistics, human resources and customer service.
- **Learning and subject connections:** Mathematics, commerce, economics, communication, data handling and collaboration can be useful foundations.
- **Course families to explore:** Commerce, business administration, management, accounting, finance, entrepreneurship, retail and logistics programmes.
- **Further exploration:** Compare the course subjects and current institution/programme status. Do not treat a course title as proof of a particular job outcome.

### law

- **Introduction:** Law explores how rules are made, interpreted and applied in society.
- **What it broadly involves:** Reading carefully, examining evidence, understanding different viewpoints and communicating reasoned arguments.
- **Activities and areas of work:** Legal research, public policy, compliance, contracts, rights awareness and dispute-resolution processes.
- **Learning and subject connections:** Language, social studies, history, political understanding, research and clear writing are useful areas to develop.
- **Course families to explore:** Integrated law degrees, law degrees after graduation, legal studies and related public-policy or social-science programmes.
- **Further exploration:** **Source-backed context:** the Bar Council of India states that it regulates legal education standards and recognition context for law degrees qualifying for enrolment. Verify the current institution, course mode and BCI information directly; do not infer professional eligibility from a course title.

### government

- **Introduction:** Government and public service looks at how public institutions, policies and services affect communities.
- **What it broadly involves:** Understanding public needs, rules, administration, information and responsible decision-making.
- **Activities and areas of work:** Public administration, community programmes, policy research, civic communication, records, planning and service delivery.
- **Learning and subject connections:** Social studies, economics, language, history, data interpretation and communication can help students explore this area.
- **Course families to explore:** Public administration, political science, social work, economics, law, development studies and related humanities programmes.
- **Further exploration:** A course can build relevant knowledge, but recruitment and examination requirements are set separately by the relevant authority and can change. No named recruitment pathway is asserted here.

### education

- **Introduction:** Education explores how learners develop knowledge, skills and confidence in schools, communities and other settings.
- **What it broadly involves:** Planning learning activities, explaining ideas, listening to learners and reflecting on what helps people understand.
- **Activities and areas of work:** Teaching, learning support, curriculum development, educational content, training and education administration.
- **Learning and subject connections:** Strong communication, patience, observation, subject knowledge and an interest in how people learn are useful foundations.
- **Course families to explore:** Education, teacher education, early childhood education, special education, training and educational technology programmes.
- **Further exploration:** **Source-backed context:** NCTE describes its norms, standards and recognition role for teacher education. Check the exact programme and current recognition information before treating any route as a teaching qualification.

### science

- **Introduction:** Science and research uses evidence, observation and testing to understand questions about the natural world, people, materials and systems.
- **What it broadly involves:** Asking questions, forming explanations, gathering information, testing methods and communicating what was found.
- **Activities and areas of work:** Laboratory work, environmental study, data analysis, fieldwork, health research, materials study and scientific communication.
- **Learning and subject connections:** Science subjects, mathematics, curiosity, careful record-keeping and logical reasoning are useful foundations.
- **Course families to explore:** Science degrees, research-oriented programmes, laboratory and technical courses, environmental studies and interdisciplinary programmes.
- **Further exploration:** Compare actual subject combinations, laboratory access, practical requirements and progression routes for each named programme. **Source-backed context:** CSIR research-fellowship guidance demonstrates that research training has programme- and eligibility-specific conditions; it does not establish an undergraduate route.

### arts-design

- **Introduction:** Arts and design explores visual expression, making, design choices and how people experience objects, spaces and messages.
- **What it broadly involves:** Observing, experimenting, developing ideas, making and improving work through feedback.
- **Activities and areas of work:** Illustration, visual communication, product ideas, user experience, craft, animation, fashion, architecture-related design and creative production.
- **Learning and subject connections:** Art, design, observation, visual communication, technology and project work can all be useful.
- **Course families to explore:** Fine arts, design, visual communication, animation, product design, fashion and architecture-related programmes.
- **Further exploration:** Portfolio, entrance, recognition and subject requirements vary widely. **Source-backed context:** CoA standards apply to architectural education, one part of this broad field; they do not validate every arts or design programme.

### media

- **Introduction:** Media explores how information, stories and messages are researched, created, presented and shared.
- **What it broadly involves:** Understanding audiences, communicating clearly, checking information, creating content and using different formats responsibly.
- **Activities and areas of work:** Journalism, video, audio, photography, editing, content design, communication, publishing, advertising and digital media.
- **Learning and subject connections:** Language, research, visual thinking, technology, communication and ethical judgement are useful areas to explore.
- **Course families to explore:** Journalism, mass communication, media studies, film, digital content, advertising, public relations and design-related programmes.
- **Further exploration:** Check the actual curriculum, equipment, practical work and institutional status. **Source-backed context:** the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting identifies public media-training institutes, including IIMC and FTII; this is not a claim about availability at another provider.

### agriculture-environment

- **Introduction:** Agriculture and environment explores food systems, plants, animals, soil, water, ecosystems and how people use natural resources.
- **What it broadly involves:** Observing living systems, understanding local conditions, testing practical approaches and considering sustainability.
- **Activities and areas of work:** Crop and soil work, horticulture, animal and fisheries systems, environmental monitoring, natural-resource management, extension and agricultural technology.
- **Learning and subject connections:** Biology, chemistry, geography, environmental science, practical observation and data collection can be useful foundations.
- **Course families to explore:** Agriculture, horticulture, forestry, fisheries, environmental science, food systems, agricultural engineering and allied-science programmes.
- **Further exploration:** **Source-backed context:** ICAR describes its role in agricultural-education planning, coordination and quality. Check the named course, university, recognition and current eligibility before applying.

### hospitality-tourism

- **Introduction:** Hospitality and tourism explores how people plan, host and support travel, accommodation, food and visitor experiences.
- **What it broadly involves:** Communication, organisation, service design, local knowledge, teamwork and attention to detail.
- **Activities and areas of work:** Hotel operations, food service, travel support, event coordination, visitor information, destination services and tourism communication.
- **Learning and subject connections:** Communication, languages, geography, business, food-related learning and teamwork can be useful foundations.
- **Course families to explore:** Hotel management, tourism studies, travel and tourism, culinary programmes, event management and service-sector skill courses.
- **Further exploration:** Check practical training, provider status, location and admission information directly. **Source-backed context:** NCVET provides vocational-qualification context, but no direct hospitality/tourism education authority was confirmed in this review; keep this field pending review.

### social-sciences

- **Introduction:** Social sciences explore how people, communities, institutions and cultures interact and change.
- **What it broadly involves:** Asking questions about society, reading evidence, listening to different perspectives and interpreting information carefully.
- **Activities and areas of work:** Psychology-related study, sociology, community work, research, development programmes, public communication, policy support and social services.
- **Learning and subject connections:** Social studies, language, research, statistics, empathy and careful analysis are useful areas to develop.
- **Course families to explore:** Psychology, sociology, social work, anthropology, economics, development studies, political science and interdisciplinary humanities programmes.
- **Further exploration:** Compare the actual curriculum and institution information rather than inferring outcomes. Avoid treating this broad field as evidence of a clinical or professional qualification.
